Printing the engine for India’s next launch vehicle

ISRO’s LME1100 programme brings additive manufacturing, high-speed turbomachinery and methane propulsion together for India’s Next Generation Launch Vehicle.

16 Oct 2026 | Indian Propulsion Engineering

Designed here. Printed differently. Tested to fly.

India’s next generation of launch vehicles will require engines that are powerful, reusable and practical to manufacture. ISRO’s answer is the LME1100—an indigenous liquid-oxygen and methane engine being developed for the Next Generation Launch Vehicle.

More than 70% of its components have been realised through additive manufacturing with Indian industry. The programme includes an additively manufactured gas generator, a 3D-printed liquid-oxygen pump impeller, Indian-made bearings and dynamic seals, spark-torch igniters and a laser-based ignition system.

On 09 Oct 2026, ISRO successfully completed the first ignition test of the full-scale gas generator. The integrated engine is targeted to be ready for hot testing in 2027.

Our perspective: printing changes how a part is made—not what it must survive. Cryogenic temperatures, extreme pressure, rapid ignition, vibration and high rotational speed still decide whether the hardware can fly.

Reference: ISRO, 16 Oct 2026

Electric Drive Engineering · Page 01

The EV drive that gets smaller by spinning faster

ZF’s high-revolution concept turns a familiar mechanical relationship into a packaging advantage: for the same power, more rotational speed means the motor can produce less torque—and therefore be built smaller.

05 Nov 2026 | Electric Powertrain

30,000 rpm changes the packaging equation

ZF has presented a compact 3-in-1 electric drive that combines the motor, inverter and transmission. Its motor reaches up to 30,000 rpm—well above the 18,000 rpm benchmark used for comparison.

The faster motor is paired with an approximately 18:1 two-stage helical transmission. Together, the system is designed to deliver up to 200 kW peak power and 3,500 Nm peak wheel torque.

≈25%smaller and lighter than the comparable drive

Engineering takeaway: compactness does not come from speed alone. It comes from designing the motor, bearings, cooling, inverter and gearing as one system.

Electric Drive Engineering · Page 02

Speed shrinks the motor. Gearing restores the torque.

Power is the product of torque and angular speed. Raise the speed and the same power can be produced with less motor torque—but the design must then manage higher centrifugal forces, heat, noise and bearing loads.

Curiosity is a skill, not a personality trait

Curiosity is not something you either have or do not have. Like any skill, it becomes stronger when we practise it.

NK
NavaneethHR Team

What curiosity does for you

People who keep asking “why?” and “how?” often reach beyond the surface of a problem. Their questions reveal assumptions, invite other perspectives and turn an everyday moment into an opportunity to learn.

Solves the real problemDig past the symptom to a root cause, so the solution lasts.
Strengthens teamsA thoughtful question shows that another person’s view matters.
Keeps you learningGenuine interest keeps skills fresh without waiting to be pushed.
Eases change“Help me understand” creates more progress than immediate resistance.
“Stay hungry. Stay foolish. Stay curious.”

Building on Steve Jobs’ timeless words: hunger fuels ambition, foolishness gives us the courage to challenge convention, and curiosity keeps us questioning the ordinary until we discover something extraordinary.

Five questions to kindle your curiosity

Tap a question to discover why
?Why do most motorcycles shift 1–N–2–3 instead of N–1–2–3?
First gear must be easy to reach positively from a stop. Placing neutral as a half-step between first and second also makes it less likely that a rider will accidentally select neutral while shifting through the driving gears.
?Why does milk boil over while water usually does not?
Milk’s proteins and fats form a film at the surface. Steam becomes trapped beneath it, lifts the film and pushes the liquid over the rim. Water releases its bubbles more freely.
?How does a fuel nozzle know when the tank is full?
A small sensing port near the nozzle tip draws in air while fuel flows. When rising fuel covers that port, the pressure changes and a mechanical diaphragm trips the shut-off valve—no electronic sensor inside the tank is required.
?Why are manhole covers usually round?
A round cover cannot fall through its own circular opening, regardless of how it is turned. It can also be rolled into position and does not require alignment with corners.
?Why do elevators often have mirrors?
Mirrors make a small enclosure feel larger, reduce discomfort while waiting, help passengers see movement around them and assist wheelchair users when entering or reversing out.

This week: ask one more question than you normally would—and see where it leads. After all, every great discovery begins with a simple question: “Why?”

Word of the Month

Aglet

AG-let
Meaning

The small sheath fixed to the end of a shoelace, cord or drawstring—usually made from plastic or metal.

Everyday engineering

An aglet compresses loose fibres into a firm, narrow tip, making the lace easier to thread through an eyelet.

Why it matters

This tiny component prevents fraying, extends the useful life of the lace and turns a flexible bundle of fibres into something easy to handle.

The uneven ropes

Two ropes each take exactly 60 minutes to burn, but neither burns uniformly. How can you measure exactly 45 minutes?

Light rope one at both ends and rope two at one end. Rope one ends after 30 minutes. Then light the other end of rope two; its remainder takes 15 minutes.
